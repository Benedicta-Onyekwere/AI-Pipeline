# Lecture 1: Introduction to the Data Pipeline and AI for CSOs
**Summary of Key Topics**

## 1. The Civic Data vs. AI Clash
*   **Civic Data Reality:** Messy, contradictory, and context-heavy (local definitions, biases).
*   **AI Assumption:** Expects standardized, clean inputs and assumes immediate utility.
*   **Result:** AI is "context-blind" and often hallucinates or misinterprets data when it encounters gaps or inconsistencies common in civic work.

## 2. How LLMs Actually Work
*   **Probability Engines:** They predict the next token (word) based on probability, not facts. They connect concepts mathematically rather than querying a database of knowledge.
*   **The Illusion:** Users often mistake text simulation for reasoning or emotion. In reality, AI can make confident but illegal moves because it mimics patterns without understanding the underlying rules.

## 3. The Data Pipeline as a Harness
*   **Safety Mechanism:** To use AI safely, you must constrain it within a rigorous 7-step process: **Define, Find, Get, Verify, Clean, Analyze, Present**.
*   **Golden Rule:** Never let an AI agent jump directly from data collection ("Get") to analysis ("Analyze"). Human verification is mandatory in between.
*   **Blindspots:** AI often "fixes" missing data (e.g., sensor gaps) by fabricating plausible numbers, unaware of the real-world context (e.g., maintenance).

## 4. The "Define" Phase (Where Projects Fail)
*   **Critical First Step:** Most projects fail here by being vague. Success requires three specific components:
    1.  **Research Question:** Specific, answerable, and decision-relevant.
    2.  **Hypothesis:** An informed expectation of the result (prevents fishing for patterns).
    3.  **Horizon Table:** An empty spreadsheet designed *before* data collection that defines the exact rows and columns needed to test the hypothesis.

## 5. AI's Role vs. Human Role
*   **AI:** Good for brainstorming columns, suggesting data sources, and identifying gaps.
*   **Human:** Must decide the research question, judge political significance, and provide the domain context that makes the project necessary. The "editorial question" is strictly human-driven.
